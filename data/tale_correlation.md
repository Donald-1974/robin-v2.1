# ROBIN v2.1 — Tale Correlation Chart
# Collation of the working corpus: fable → household tale → literary fairy tale → medieval Otherworld → Celtic sovereignty
# Manuscript always outranks the chart.

## I. The five laws (do not mix without a signal)

1. AESOP LAW — one action, one reversal, one portable maxim. Animals are types. Justice is a proverb.
2. HOUSEHOLD LAW (Grimm / Perrault / Jacobs) — kin, hunger, forest or kitchen, three trials, often cruel restoration of a folk order.
3. ANDERSEN LAW — folk furniture + modern remainder. Body is billed. Ending may refuse comic rescue. Grief is not a lesson.
4. MEDIEVAL OTHERWORLD LAW — fairy is a jurisdiction. Plot is a contract (geis, rash promise, year-and-a-day, forbidden day). Time shears the mortal life.
5. CELTIC SOVEREIGNTY LAW — the land is female. Rule is a marriage or a cup. Otherworld opens inside the royal site. Beauty after the kiss is recognition, not a makeover.

## IV. Correlation rules for Alicyn’s draft

- If the tale ends on a sentence you could stitch on a sampler, it is Aesop-law even if the furniture is fairy.
- If a child dies and the city does not stop, it is Andersen-law. Do not bolt a maxim onto it.
- If a woman gives arms, gold, or a cup and the man loses everything by speaking, it is Otherworld-contract, not “romance.”
- If the ugly woman becomes beautiful after the right man accepts her, check whether the land just recognized a king. If yes, Celtic sovereignty. If no, loathly-lady entertainment.
- If time in the hill does not wreck the parish calendar, the Otherworld is costume.
- Grimm restores a folk order. Celtic sovereignty can leave the king already dethroned while he still wears the torque.
- Do not stamp “sovereignty goddess” on every strong woman. Steal the cup and the clause, not the label.

## V. One-line keys

Aesop: the world fits in a proverb.
Grimm: the house and the forest keep accounts.
Andersen: the account cannot be closed.
Medieval fairy: another court has standing.
Celtic sovereignty: the land is the plaintiff, the wife, and the drink.
